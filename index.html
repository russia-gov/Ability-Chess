Abilities Guide

Captures earn points equal to the captured piece's standard value. Spend those points on one ability before making your normal move.

Shield 3 points

Protect one of your pieces from ordinary capture until your next turn begins. The shield is immediately lost if that piece moves. Bomb is not an ordinary capture.

Freeze 3 points

Choose an enemy non-king piece. It cannot move during its owner’s next turn, but it can still be captured or affected by other abilities.

Bomb 4 points

Destroy all pieces in a 3×3 area, including the initiating piece. Neither king may be in the blast, and Fortify blocks the effect.

Swap 4 points

Exchange the squares of two of your non-king pieces. The swap cannot leave your king under attack.

Recall 4 points

Return your most recently moved non-king piece to the square from which it moved, provided the piece is still there and the old square is empty.

Ambush 4 points

Trap one of your non-king pieces. If it is captured before your next turn, the capturing piece is destroyed as well.

Fortify 4 points

Protect a 2×2 area containing your king. Enemy abilities cannot affect your pieces in that area until your next turn.

Teleport 4 points

Move one non-king piece to any empty, unfortified square. Pawns may only be teleported while on their own half and only to another square on their own half. Teleport counts as your move and ends your turn. It cannot expose your king or place the teleported piece where it immediately attacks the enemy king.

Reinforce 5 points

Immediately promote one pawn beyond the halfway line to a knight or bishop, then make your normal move.

Portal 5 points

Choose two empty squares. The portal pair remains active for three of its owner’s turns, including the turn it is created. During those turns, any White or Black piece entering either portal exits from the other when the exit is empty. Both directions work for both players. Creating the portal does not consume the normal move.

Double Move 6 points

Make two normal chess moves in one turn. If you begin in check, the first move may leave the king in check provided the second move legally resolves it. Neither move may capture the opposing king.

Permanent movement upgrades

Upgrades permanently expand movement and differ from one-use abilities. Buying one costs material-bank points and consumes the entire turn. It affects every friendly piece of that type, including promoted pieces of that resulting type. Each player may own upgrades for up to the game’s selected number of piece types and may have only one named upgrade active per type. A new purchase may replace an owned upgrade: choose the upgrade to remove, pay the new upgrade’s full cost, and consume the turn. There are no refunds. Ownership remains even if every current piece of that type is captured. Purple markers identify upgraded pieces; hover over one to review its movement.

Vanguard 4 points · Pawn

Every pawn may move one square diagonally forward into an empty square without capturing. Normal captures remain unchanged. Useful for slipping around blocked files.

Reverse Gear 4 points · Pawn

Every pawn may move one square directly backward into an empty square. It cannot capture backward or regain a two-square move. Promotion remains on its normal rank. Useful for reorganizing pawn structure.

Veteran 6 points · Pawn

Every pawn may move one empty square and capture diagonally toward either end. The original-direction two-square move and en passant remain unchanged; promotion occurs only on the opponent-facing promotion rank; reaching the pawn’s own back rank never promotes it. Creates flexible two-way pressure.

Lancer 5 points · Knight

Every knight additionally moves or captures one square diagonally. Standard knight moves remain. Helps knights operate in cramped positions.

Charger 6 points · Knight

Every knight additionally moves or captures one square in its owner's normal forward direction. This is one ordinary move, not an extra step. Adds direct forward pressure.

Cardinal 6 points · Bishop

Every bishop additionally moves or captures one square orthogonally while retaining diagonal movement. Gives bishops close-range access to either color complex.

Color Shift 5 points · Bishop

Every bishop may move exactly one square orthogonally only into an empty square; it cannot capture with this move. It changes square color and consumes the normal turn. Repositions a bishop onto the other color complex.

Archbishop 8 points · Bishop

Every bishop additionally moves and captures like a knight while retaining diagonal movement. Produces powerful mixed sliding and leaping threats.

Bastion 6 points · Rook

Every rook may pass through exactly one friendly piece during a normal rook line and land on an empty square beyond it or capture an enemy piece there. It cannot jump an enemy or pass more than one piece. Helps rooks escape blocked files.

Turret 6 points · Rook

Every rook additionally moves or captures one square diagonally while retaining normal rook movement. Adds close-range diagonal control.

Chancellor 9 points · Rook

Every rook additionally moves and captures like a knight while retaining orthogonal movement. Creates a highly mobile compound attacker.

Phase Step 7 points · Queen

Every queen may pass through exactly one friendly piece on a normal queen line and land on an empty square beyond it or capture an enemy piece there. It cannot jump an enemy or pass more than one piece. Lets the queen escape friendly congestion.

Royal Step 7 points · King

The king additionally moves and captures like a knight, but may never land on an attacked square. It is not castling. Offers tactical escapes and surprise captures.

Escape Route 6 points · King

The king additionally moves exactly two squares horizontally, vertically, or diagonally. It cannot begin in attack; the intermediate square must be empty and safe; the destination must be safe and may capture. It is not castling and moves no rook. Enables carefully screened long escapes.

Core scoring: pawn 1, knight 3, bishop 3, rook 5, queen 9. Games may end by checkmate or king capture. Stalemate is not declared while the player has a legal ability escape. Draws also include agreement, threefold repetition, the fifty-move rule, and insufficient material. You may use at most one ability per turn.
